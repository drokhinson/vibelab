Two widths, one height

The feed card has no fixed size at all today. Width comes from one of three variants (full-width solo, 260px landscape strip, 220px portrait strip) and height comes from whatever aspect the decoded image happens to have — so every session is a different shape. Below: what's on screen now, then what shipped — and the two one-size options it was weighed against. The collection-status pill is gone from all three options. The controls stress the frames — Extreme swaps in a 9:16 phone shot and a 21:9 panorama, Long swaps in the kind of subtitled game name that used to fight the pill for room in the meta row.

Where we are

the problem

Three width variants multiplied by a free-running photo height. A solo play with a tall snapshot is over 500px of card; the same play inside a two-game session is 220px wide with a squat letterboxed cover. Nothing lines up, and the rail has to centre its cards vertically because neighbours never match.

A

Two widths, one height

shipped

The combination that beats all three of the others. Keep two footprints, so a landscape photo gets horizontal room and a portrait photo gets vertical room — but give both cards the same 230px photo-frame height, and fill whatever the image doesn't cover with a blurred, dimmed copy of itself. A tile is 280 × 321 or 208 × 321: one height across the whole feed, two widths, and nothing ever cropped. A landscape shot blurs above and below, a portrait shot blurs down each side, and box art at ~0.78 against the tall tile's 0.80 frame gets hairlines. The full-width solo variant retires — a solo play is one tile, centred.

widths 2 heights 1 cropping none rail one baseline
B

One footprint — fill the frame

also considered

One tile: 264 × 270, a 4:3 frame, every image centre-cropped to fill it. Perfectly uniform, and the rail finally lines up on a single baseline. The price is real: a portrait snapshot loses exactly half its height, and box art gets its title bar and publisher strip sliced off — which is exactly the artwork people recognise a game by.

widths 1 heights 1 tall images half cropped box art title cropped
C

One footprint — fit on its own blur

also considered

Contributed the blur-fill mechanic that A ships. Same single 264 × 270 tile and same 4:3 frame as B — but the image sits whole inside it, over a blurred, dimmed, scaled-up copy of itself. This is the answer to "I'm not sure how to best fit that": the frame is uniform, and nothing is ever cropped — a wide photo, a tall photo and a portrait box cover all land in the same box with their full composition intact, each carrying its own colour into the letterbox so the card still reads as a photograph rather than as art on a grey plate. Its one weakness is what sent us to A: with a single frame a 9:16 shot shrinks to a narrow sliver, where a tall tile gives it the full height.

On the pill. Removing it takes the meta row down to one thing — the winner — so it gets the full card width and stops fighting an ellipsis on a 220px tile. It also removes the only reason the feed needed a per-game collection map on screen, and the only tap target on the card that wasn't "open this game" or "flip this card". Every other surface that shows owned / played / wishlist — the collection grid, wishlist, game detail, the hot-games rail — keeps its pill untouched.
