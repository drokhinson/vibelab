A phone on its side is not a small iPad

The iPad rail is the right instinct, but the tier that decides when to use it is wrong. Today the app picks a tier by width — and an iPhone 15 held sideways is 852 pt wide, which lands it in the tablet tier and keeps the bottom bar. Width alone cannot tell an iPad standing up (768 × 1024) from a phone lying down (852 × 393). Height can.

What the chrome costs, sideways

Today — header 53px + bottom bar 64px117 of 393px
Proposed — both folded into a 64px rail0 of 393px

Thirty percent of the short axis, handed back to content. The rail spends 64px of the long axis instead — 7% of 852 — which is the trade the whole idea rests on.

What follows the iPad

  • The bar becomes a left rail and the global header folds into it — logo on top, three tabs, Alerts and Settings sunk to the bottom. Exactly the wide tier's structure.
  • Screens split into two panes. Feed gets its stream plus the Hot this week / Buddies sidebar; the play cascade gets its form plus the live roster. Same split the tablet tier already ships.
  • Thumbs are already there. Held sideways, thumbs rest at the left and right edges, not the bottom middle. The rail lands under the left one.

What has to differ from the iPad

  • The rail must be compact. The wide tier's rail is 88px with a 64px brand and five 64px tabs — 396px of height, more than a landscape iPhone has. This one is 64px wide with 46px tabs: 268px, and it fits.
  • Panes scroll on their own. The tablet tier scrolls the page as one piece; at 393px tall that drags the sidebar off screen the moment you read past the second play. Each pane takes its own scroll instead.
  • Play cards turn with the phone. A 4:3 photo card shows one play per screen sideways. Photo left, result right shows three — and it is the same card, re-flowed, not a new component.
  • No 1040px column. The wide tier centres a column and lets the monitor keep the rest. Here there is no rest — content fills the width beside the rail.
  • The rail sits inside the notch. Sideways, the sensor housing eats the left edge. The rail offsets by env(safe-area-inset-left), which needs viewport-fit=cover — a change index.html has a standing note against making casually, and the one real prerequisite here.

The rule that picks it

tier = "land" when (orientation: landscape) and (max-height: 560px) and (min-width: 640px)

560px clears every phone in landscape (393–430) and stays well under an iPad standing up (1024). 640px keeps a small phone that is still too narrow for two panes — an SE at 667×375 squeaks in, a folded foldable does not — on the bottom bar. Above it all, the existing width tiers are untouched, so nothing about the iPad or the desktop changes.
