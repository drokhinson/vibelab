Scoring a game whose expansions both add and replace

Everdell on the table with three boxes ticked: Pearlbrook and Newleaf are add-ons, Everdell Legacy reprints the whole score sheet. Four frames, in the order the host meets them. Everything below is the shipped CSS and the shipped class names — the scorepad here is the scorepad.

add_on

The expansion brings extra categories to a scorepad that is otherwise the base game's. Its rows are appended, in ascending BGG id, and each one draws the expansion's colour down its left edge.

replace

The expansion is the score sheet. It becomes the scorepad by default — but it does not lock the base game out: both become pills, and the host can pick either.

The scoring card, frame by frame

Only the base game's grid and the replacements are ever pills. Add-ons are not alternatives to anything, so they never appear as an option — they fold into whichever pill is chosen, and say so on their own rows.

what this surfaces

The composed title does not fit at two add-ons. Frames 2 and 4 read “Everdell score sheet + Pearlbrook +” — Newleaf is ellipsised off the end of a one-line bar on a 390px phone. composedTitle() only collapses to a count at three extras, which makes exactly-two the longest string it can produce and the one that always truncates. The composition is still legible from the coloured rows, so nothing is lost — but the bar reads as broken text. Collapsing at two (“Everdell score sheet + 2 expansions”), or naming only the scorepad and letting the rows carry the rest, both fix it; neither is in this change.

Two facts, two channels

A row header carries a colour wash and a left rule, and they answer different questions. Collapsing them into one mark would make a green Pearlbrook row unable to be both green and Pearlbrook's.

The wash — what the row is
14% of the palette slug the grid's author picked for that row (green, gold, …). Their tint for the category, resolved by the stylesheet because the scorepad is paper in both themes and an author could otherwise pick a yellow nobody can read.
The left rule — where it came from
The contributing expansion's own colour (games.expansion_color), matching its pill's dot and its dot in the reference guide. Rows from whichever grid leads the composition carry none — marking those too would say the whole table came from somewhere else.

Where the mode comes from

Not from the host at the table — from the author of the grid, who has the box in front of them. It reads back as a badge wherever someone is deciding whether to adopt a grid: the offer sheet, the reference-guide scroll, the browse pool.
